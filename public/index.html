Guide

Get your part-time work permit in 4 steps

Called 시간제 취업 허가. Working without it can mean a fine of up to ₩10 million and future visa restrictions.

Find a job & get the contract

Get a written contract with hourly wage, hours and duties, plus the employer's business registration.

Visit your international office

Your school signs the part-time work confirmation form. Bring your TOPIK certificate and transcript.

Apply on HiKorea

e-Application → "Permission for Part-Time Work of International Students". Or apply at the immigration office.

Wait for approval, then start

Start only after the permit is granted — working while it's under review counts as unpermitted work.

Usually allowed

  • Cafés, restaurants, convenience stores
  • Tutoring & language teaching assistance (not private academy instructor roles)
  • Translation, interpreting, tour guiding
  • Office work, on-campus jobs, events & promotions

Not allowed

  • Bars, clubs and other entertainment / adult venues
  • Construction sites
  • Manufacturing — restricted, check with your school first
  • Any job without a permit, or more hours than permitted
FAQ

Questions students ask us

Can I start working as soon as I arrive in Korea?

Degree students (D-2) can usually apply once enrolled. Korean language students (D-4) must have stayed in Korea for at least 6 months first. Either way, you need the permit before the first shift.

Do foreign students get the same minimum wage?

Yes. The 2026 minimum wage of ₩10,320/hour applies to everyone working in Korea, regardless of nationality or visa. It rises to ₩10,700 on January 1, 2027.

What is 주휴수당 (weekly holiday allowance)?

If you work 15 or more hours a week and complete your scheduled days, your employer must pay you for one extra "rest day". At 20 hrs/week that's about 4 extra paid hours each week. Use our pay calculator above.

Can I work more than one part-time job?

Yes, but each workplace must be listed on your permit and your combined hours must stay within your limit.

What if my employer refuses to sign a contract or pays late?

A written contract is required by law. You can get free help in many languages from the Ministry of Employment and Labor (call 1350) or a Foreign Workers Support Center.
